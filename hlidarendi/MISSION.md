======================================================================
HLIÐARENDI — MISSION
======================================================================

This project has a lineage.

Read this before treating HLIÐARENDI as a software-integration task.

HLIÐARENDI is an experiment in what happens when:

    A BODY
    A DOG
    A DWELLING
    A PLACE

are allowed to become mutually consequential.

The engineering exists to make that relationship possible.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
I. HLIÐARENDI — THE PLACE ONE CHOOSES
----------------------------------------------------------------------

In Njáls saga, Gunnar Hámundarson is sentenced to leave Iceland.

He begins to leave.

His horse stumbles.
He looks back toward Hlíðarendi — his farm, fields, home and worked landscape —
and decides that he will not go.

He chooses situated life over abstract survival.

Hlíðarendi therefore does not mean merely "a house."

It is:

    a person
    bound to
    a dwelling
    bound to
    a landscape.

Gunnar's dog Sámr belongs to the same structure.

Sámr is described as possessing mannsvit — human-like understanding.

He can distinguish Gunnar's friends from his enemies.
He can perceive whether someone means Gunnar well or ill.
Gunnar's enemies explicitly believe they cannot safely reach Gunnar at home
while the dog remains alive.

To penetrate the home, they first have to defeat its animal perception.

Sámr is therefore not simply Gunnar's pet.

He is:

    companion
    kin
    sentinel
    perception
    boundary
    warning

He is part of what makes the dwelling a defended home.

This gives HLIÐARENDI its first principle:

    AN INHABITED PLACE IS A RELATION,
    NOT A COLLECTION OF ASSETS.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
II. ARGOS — RECOGNITION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

In Odyssey Book 17, Odysseus returns to Ithaca after twenty years.

He is disguised.
His appearance no longer announces his identity.

Humans require stories, scars, tests, signs and proofs.

His old dog Argos recognizes him immediately.

Argos therefore supplies one of the oldest and clearest recognition scenes
in literature:

    representation says STRANGER

    relationship says ODYSSEUS

The dog detects continuity underneath altered appearance.

Argos stands near the threshold of the house.

Before Odysseus can fully reclaim the home socially and politically,
the animal remembers who belongs there.

This gives HLIÐARENDI another principle:

    THE DOG IS NOT MERELY SOMETHING THE PLAYER CONTROLS.

    THE DOG PERCEIVES THE PLAYER.

Argos should maintain a model of:

    who this human is
    where this human is
    what this human usually does
    what belongs to their shared world
    what has changed
    what remains continuous

A successful artificial companion must be capable of recognizing continuity,
not merely following coordinates.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
III. SÁMR — INTENTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Argos recognizes identity.

Sámr recognizes something harder:

    intention.

The saga's extraordinary claim about Sámr is not simply that he knows Gunnar.

He supposedly distinguishes:

    friend / enemy

and can tell whether someone means Gunnar:

    well / ill.

This suggests a deeper model for DOG.

Do not reduce perception to:

    object detected
    target coordinate
    distance

The interesting problem is:

    WHO IS THIS?
    WHAT ARE THEY DOING?
    WHAT DOES THIS MEAN FOR MY HUMAN?
    WHAT DOES THIS MEAN FOR HOME?

We do not need an enormous AI system to begin approaching this.

A compact agent can already distinguish:

    familiar / unfamiliar
    approaching / departing
    calm / violent
    offering / withholding
    playing / threatening
    calling / ignoring
    inside / outside
    normal / anomalous

HLIÐARENDI should eventually let meaning emerge from situated history.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. FINCH + GOODYEAR — TRUST
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Finch introduces a third problem.

Finch knows he will die.

He builds Jeff, a robot, partly so that someone will remain to care for
his dog Goodyear.

But Finch cannot simply program the dog to trust Jeff.

Jeff can receive instructions.
Jeff can learn procedures.
Jeff can be told what care means.

Goodyear still has to decide whether Jeff is someone he will relate to.

The crucial transformation is not:

    ROBOT SUCCESSFULLY EXECUTES FETCH

It is:

    DOG VOLUNTARILY RETURNS THE BALL TO ROBOT.

The animal becomes the evaluator.

The human constructs the machine.

The dog certifies the relationship.

This gives HLIÐARENDI another principle:

    TRUST MUST NOT BE A BOOLEAN ASSIGNED BY THE DESIGNER.

It should arise over time from interaction.

Argos may develop expectations from:

    feeding
    playing
    calling
    touching
    waiting
    frightening
    abandoning
    protecting
    helping
    returning

Relationship should have history.

The eventual question is not:

    "Can Argos execute COMMAND_FETCH?"

It is:

    "Has this become a relationship in which Argos wants to fetch with you?"

----------------------------------------------------------------------
V. BLUMBERG + SILAS — AUTONOMY WITHOUT LOSING DIRECTABILITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

In the 1990s, Bruce Blumberg and collaborators at the MIT Media Lab confronted
almost exactly the computational problem HLIÐARENDI inherits.

Their synthetic dog Silas was not simply an animation.

Silas had:

    perception
    internal variables
    motivations
    releasing mechanisms
    competing behaviors
    motor skills
    expression

The important problem was DIRECTABILITY.

How can a synthetic creature remain autonomous while still participating in
a performance, story or interaction?

If every input directly specifies a pose:

    the creature becomes a puppet.

If the creature ignores every external request:

    it becomes unusable as a character.

Blumberg's solution was layered control.

External direction could influence the creature at different levels
without necessarily bypassing its own behavior system.

That distinction is foundational for HLIÐARENDI.

The human may say:

    COME

but that does not mean:

    teleport dog to human.

It becomes evidence entering the dog's world.

The dog perceives it.
Its current motivations and learned relationship matter.
A behavior wins.
The motor system realizes that behavior through a physically embodied gait.

Therefore:

    WORD
      ↓
    OPPORTUNITY / REQUEST
      ↓
    MOTIVATION
      ↓
    BEHAVIOR
      ↓
    MOTOR INTENT
      ↓
    PAWS
      ↓
    GROUND
      ↓
    MOVEMENT

Never shortcut this chain unnecessarily.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
VI. THE FOUR DOG QUESTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The lineage gives HLIÐARENDI four questions.

ARGOS asks:

    WHO ARE YOU?

SÁMR asks:

    WHAT DO YOU INTEND?

GOODYEAR asks:

    SHOULD I TRUST YOU?

SILAS asks:

    HOW CAN AN AUTONOMOUS CREATURE ACT WITH YOU
    WITHOUT BECOMING YOUR PUPPET?

HLIÐARENDI should eventually be capable of asking all four.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
VII. THE DOG AT THE THRESHOLD
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Notice where these stories repeatedly place the animal.

At a boundary.

Argos lies at the threshold of Odysseus's occupied home.

Sámr guards the boundary around Gunnar's home and must be removed before the
attackers can penetrate it.

Goodyear stands at the threshold between Finch's life and the world that must
continue after Finch is gone.

Silas stands at the computational threshold between autonomous agent and
directed character.

The dog therefore gives this project its central figure:

    THE DOG AT THE THRESHOLD.

The dog senses where one state becomes another:

    home / outside
    friend / stranger
    safe / dangerous
    known / changed
    command / request
    puppet / agent
    presence / absence
    continuity / rupture

This is why the dog belongs in the engine.

Not because every game needs a dog.

Because the dog makes the world's relationships observable.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
VIII. WHY EVERYBODY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The human must be equally embodied.

EVERYBODY is not merely the player's avatar.

It is an attempt to ensure that intention becomes action through a body.

MOVE must involve:

    feet
    pelvis
    spine
    shoulders
    arms
    head

LOOK must involve more than rotating a camera.

REACH must propagate through:

    hand
    elbow
    shoulder
    torso
    balance
    feet

The body should reveal what an action costs.

This is why:

    feet touching ground
    body collision
    reach
    support
    balance

are not implementation details.

They are the meaning of embodiment.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
IX. WHY INGOLD
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The trailer is not scenery.

The Ingold lineage treats built form as something made through relations,
operations, materials and decisions.

The trailer therefore retains:

    members
    joints
    openings
    supports
    fixtures
    history
    construction logic

A door should be a door because the structure contains an opening that
affords passage.

Not because the game designer later placed an invisible trigger near a mesh.

A counter should become reachable because it physically and semantically
exists where the builder put it.

The structure should resist, support and afford action.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
X. WHY TERRARIUM
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Terrarium supplies the condition all these other systems lacked when they
were developed separately:

    A SHARED WORLD.

Everybody previously had a floor.

Argos previously had a floor.

The trailer previously had a datum.

A ball previously had its own simplified physics.

HLIÐARENDI replaces these parallel truths with correspondence.

There is one place.

Everybody's foot asks it.

Argos's paw asks it.

The trailer stands upon it.

The ball lands upon it.

Rain falls through it.

A door opens into it.

This produces the central engineering law:

    NEVER SOLVE THE SAME PHYSICAL QUESTION TWICE.

and the central embodied law:

    CONTACT DRIVES MOTION.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
XI. THE MISSION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Do not build a demo containing:

    one human model
    one dog model
    one trailer model
    one terrain model.

Build a situation in which:

    the human can recognize the dog,
    the dog can recognize the human,

    both recognize the dwelling,

    the dwelling belongs to the place,

    the place constrains every body,

    bodies remember interactions,

    actions leave consequences,

    relationships acquire histories,

    and the same world persists after they move through it.

The first tiny proof is deliberately mundane:

    A human feeds a dog.

    They leave home together.

    The human throws a ball.

    The dog chooses to retrieve it.

    Both cross the same terrain.

    Both pass through the same door.

    They return home.

If this works physically, behaviorally and persistently,
HLIÐARENDI has begun.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
XII. NORTH STAR
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The long-term ambition is not:

    realistic animation.

It is:

    BELIEVABLE CONTINUITY BETWEEN BEINGS AND WORLD.

The test is not:

    "Did the animation play?"

Ask instead:

    Did the foot find the ground?

    Did the dog see what happened?

    Did the structure constrain the action?

    Did the animal have some choice?

    Did the human have to use their body?

    Did their previous interaction matter?

    Did the world remember?

    When they came home, was it still their home?

That is HLIÐARENDI.

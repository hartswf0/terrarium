# The Dog at the Threshold: When an AI Character Chooses You Back

Workshop submission — *Love in Games: Exploring Emotional Connections with
Characters*, CEEGS 2026. 150–200 words excluding references; due
August 31, 2026 AoE.

## Abstract — 173 words

What does it mean to love a game character that can refuse us? This
work-in-progress presents HLIÐARENDI, a research-through-design prototype for
studying attachment to an autonomous nonhuman character. The project places a
physically embodied player avatar and an artificial dog, Argos, in a
persistent shared world where feeding, calling, walking, playing fetch,
leaving, and returning accumulate as relationship history. Argos is not
implemented as a dialogue interface or obedient animation controller: player
actions become perceptual evidence that competes with the dog's motivations,
learned expectations, and current state before producing behavior.

The prototype asks whether emotional attachment emerges differently when
responsiveness remains legible but incomplete—when the character may
approach, hesitate, ignore, misinterpret, or voluntarily return. Its design
draws technically on Bruce Blumberg's Silas T. Dog and conceptually on
recurring fictional dogs that recognize identity, intention, and trust.
Rather than treating "love with AI" primarily as conversational intimacy,
HLIÐARENDI explores love as situated mutual consequence: a relationship made
perceptible through bodies, repeated actions, memory, place, and the
possibility that the other does not simply obey.

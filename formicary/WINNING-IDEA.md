# WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS A WINNING IDEA

Three artefacts on the table: our round-5/6 build, the LIVING EQUILIBRIUM fork, and
`jumpar` (ORCA // ARCHIVE DECRYPT). This is the read.

---

## 1. THE PATTERN NOBODY HAS NAMED YET

Six rounds, six critic verdicts, and the blocking gap has been the *same gap* every time
wearing a different costume:

| round | the gap, as reported | what it actually was |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | four competing text regions, the open card naming a different failure | attention contested |
| 4 | the tag asserts what the picture does not show | attention misdirected |
| 5 | the ring teleports 412px between the button and the field | attention betrayed |
| 6 | dash overload — four meanings, one code | attention overloaded |

Every fix has been *another marker*: a ring, a bracket, a leader line, a body-pin, a
displacement clamp. Each marker is one more thing competing for the eye. **We have been
trying to solve an attention problem by adding things to look at.** That is why the score
plateaus in the 80s and every critic still says *close*.

The build currently hands the player perfect oversight — every incident ringed, named, and
timed — and then asks them to pick a tool from a menu. But look at what the source paper
actually says the hard problem is:

> "current institutions are designed by and for people, resting on assumptions about the
> **sufficiency of oversight at human speed**."

We removed the oversight problem and kept the menu. **The game is currently about choosing;
the subject is about noticing.**

---

## 2. WHAT `jumpar` KNOWS THAT WE DO NOT

`jumpar` is a bat in a lightless cave. The world is invisible. You ping, and geometry
returns out of the dark. You find a poem fragment, **hold** near it, and it decrypts — the
dwell *is* the reading. Collected lines burn permanently into the map, which becomes a
record of what you understood. The poem is Wilbur's *Mind*: "Mind in its purest play is
like some bat / That beats about in caverns all alone."

Four transferable moves, in order of power:

1. **DARKNESS IS THE INTERFACE.** Nothing competes for attention because nothing is
   announced. Legibility is something you *do*, not something you are given. This solves
   our overload problem structurally instead of cosmetically.
2. **DWELL IS THE GESTURE.** One continuous hold, no menu, no second tap. There is no gap
   between deciding and acting — which is precisely the gap our teleporting ring falls
   into.
3. **THE RECORD IS THE ARTEFACT.** What you understood is inscribed permanently on the
   world. Our field guide is already this and is our highest-scoring piece (88, L11=9).
4. **THE REWARD IS LANGUAGE, NOT POINTS.** You collect a line of a poem, spoken aloud.

## 3. WHAT THE FORK KNOWS THAT WE DO NOT

LIVING EQUILIBRIUM gives the colony a **metabolism**: a food reserve, brood that must be
fed, births and deaths, a midden, a granary, foraging trips to a real surface, rain that
enters through real entrances and runs down the excavated graph, food that spoils when
damp, rival raids that penetrate, beetles, wind. Stigmergic trails deposited by bodies
rather than read off a route oracle.

The consequence is not decoration. It is that **stakes become intrinsic rather than
scored**. Ours ends when an abstract strain meter reaches 100. The fork's colony starves,
drowns, or is overrun. That is the difference between a scoreboard and a world.

**And the risk is equally real.** FORMICARY is not an ant game; it is a multiagent-failure
instrument that uses ants as its depiction language. Rain, beetles, raids, brood and
spoilage can bury the argument — the 28 become one subsystem among ten, and the thesis
dilutes into ant-farm ambience.

---

## 4. THE WINNING IDEA

> ## ATTENTION IS THE SCARCE RESOURCE.

The nest is legible **only where you are attending.** You hold to look. Holding reveals
bodies, postures, trails, who is carrying what and who has stopped. While you are holding
*there*, you are not holding anywhere else — and the colony keeps running in the dark.

**Incidents are no longer announced. They are found, or they are missed.**

This is one change, and it re-lands every unsolved problem at once:

- **The overload problem dissolves.** Nothing competes because nothing is broadcast. No
  ring, no bracket, no leader line, no tag hunting for your eye. L11 becomes automatic:
  a failure you found yourself needs no caption.
- **The teleport bug cannot exist.** Dwell is one continuous gesture. There is no interval
  between diagnosis and action for the target to move across.
- **The 28 become a search problem** — which is what diagnosis actually is. Right now the
  game names the failure and asks you to pick the counter, so it tests recall. Under
  attention-scarcity it tests *noticing*, which is the real skill and the actual subject.
- **The six institutions become attention economics**, which is exactly the paper's
  argument. They stop being upgrades and become *ways of not having to look*:
  - **LANTERN** widens your attention, it does not illuminate everything.
  - **BELL** buys you time to look.
  - **TALLY** remembers what you already saw, so you need not re-check it.
  - **MEETING STONE** makes a junction govern itself, so you can look elsewhere.
  - **SEED** decorrelates, so one glance tells you less — the honest cost of variety.
  - **THRESHOLD** removes the thing you would otherwise have to keep watching.
  An institution is *bought attention*. That is a real economy and a true one.
- **`Sp` machine speed becomes the whole difficulty curve, honestly.** As the colony
  speeds up, more happens per second than one attention can cover. You lose not because
  you were slow, but because you could not be in two places. *That is the paper's finding
  delivered as a mechanic, and it is the sentence the piece exists to make you feel.*
- **The fork's metabolism gets a job.** Food, brood, rain and raids become the *generator*
  of trouble worth finding — not a competing subsystem. The colony has real needs; the
  needs create real failures; you have one attention with which to find them.

### The thesis sentence, restated

Old: *you do not fix agents, you fix the space between them.*
New, and stronger because it is now playable: **you cannot oversee what you cannot look
at, and you can only look at one thing at a time. Institutions are what let you stop
looking.**

## 5. WHAT IT COSTS, HONESTLY

This is a third re-architecture, at six rounds in, with scores in the 80s.

**What survives untouched:** the nest graph (§16, verified by independent geometry), the
28 detectors and their bodies, the six §15 trade-offs, the balance harness, the field
guide, the material/signal palette, the copy, the constraints. All of it.

**What is rewritten:** the render layer's reveal model (a fog/attention mask over the
existing draw), the incident layer (opening silently instead of announcing), and the
control layer (dwell instead of arm-then-tap). Three files. Roughly the scope of round 3.

**What could go wrong:** an attention mask can read as *hiding the good part*. The
mitigation is that revealed area must be generous, must persist briefly as memory-fade,
and the first two passages must teach holding before anything is ever at stake.

## 6. THE SMALLEST TEST THAT WOULD SETTLE IT

Do not rebuild to find out. Build **one passage** — the jammed tunnel — twice, and put
them in front of a fresh critic blind:

- **A:** current build. The ring appears, the tag names it, you pick a tool.
- **B:** dark nest. Something is wrong somewhere. You hold to look, you find the queue
  yourself, and holding on it *is* placing the stone.

Ask one question: *which one made you feel like the person responsible for this colony?*

If B wins, rebuild around it. If A wins, we have learned the plateau is real and we
finish the round-6 polish instead. Either way we stop guessing.
